# Euler angles and gimbal lock

:::{admonition} In plain words
:class: aerl-plain
Roll, pitch and yaw are the most natural way to describe which way a drone
points. But they have a built-in blind spot: when the nose points straight up
or down (pitch ±90°), roll and yaw start turning the drone the *same* way, so
one direction of rotation can no longer be described. This is **gimbal lock**.
It is a mathematical fact, not a bug, and it is why this project's estimators
use **quaternions** instead.
:::

```{localvideo} theory/v1_rotations.mp4
:title: Euler angles, gimbal lock, and quaternions
```

## The key idea

An attitude is built from three turns in sequence: **yaw** $\psi$ about the
vertical axis, then **pitch** $\theta$ about the new sideways axis, then
**roll** $\phi$ about the new forward axis. Multiplying the three rotations
gives the body-to-world rotation:

$$
R(\phi,\theta,\psi) = R_z(\psi)\,R_y(\theta)\,R_x(\phi).
$$

A gyroscope does *not* measure how fast these three angles change. It measures
the body's spin rate $\boldsymbol\omega$. Converting one into the other takes
a matrix that contains $\tan\theta$ and $1/\cos\theta$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\phi\\ \dot\theta\\ \dot\psi\end{bmatrix}
= E(\phi,\theta)\,\boldsymbol\omega,
\qquad E \text{ contains } \tan\theta \text{ and } \frac{1}{\cos\theta}.
$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\phi, \theta, \psi$ | roll, pitch, yaw (rad) |
| $\boldsymbol\omega$ | body spin rate measured by the gyro (rad/s) |
| $\dot\phi, \dot\theta, \dot\psi$ | how fast the three angles change (rad/s) |
| $R_x, R_y, R_z$ | elementary rotations about x, y, z |

As the pitch approaches 90°, $\cos\theta \to 0$, so $1/\cos\theta$ explodes.
An ordinary spin rate turns into an absurd rate of change of roll and yaw. In
the simulator, a steady 1 rad/s spin becomes about **17,000 °/s** of roll and
yaw rate just 0.1° from vertical:

```{figure} /_static/figures/gimbal_lock.png
:width: 640px

Top: the Euler-angle rates produced by a *constant* body rate blow up near
90° pitch. Bottom: converting the rotation back into angles jumps past 90°.
Generated by {func}`~drone_sim.visualization.gimbal_lock.plot_gimbal_lock`.
```

At exactly 90°, the rotation only depends on $\psi - \phi$: roll and yaw have
merged into one, and a degree of freedom is gone. No other choice of three
angles avoids this somewhere. The cure is the **quaternion** (four numbers
with one constraint), described on the {doc}`quaternions` page.

## Show the derivations

::::{dropdown} Derivation: from body rates to Euler-angle rates (the matrices W and E)
:icon: unfold

Add the three rate vectors, each expressed in body axes:

- $\dot\phi$ acts about the body $x$ axis directly: $[\dot\phi, 0, 0]^\top$.
- $\dot\theta$ acts about the intermediate $y$ axis, which the roll rotation
  has not yet moved into body axes: $R_x(\phi)^\top [0, \dot\theta, 0]^\top$.
- $\dot\psi$ acts about the world $z$ axis, seen through both later rotations:
  $R_x(\phi)^\top R_y(\theta)^\top [0, 0, \dot\psi]^\top$.

Multiplying out:

$$
\boldsymbol\omega =
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & -\sin\theta\\
0 & \cos\phi & \sin\phi\cos\theta\\
0 & -\sin\phi & \cos\phi\cos\theta
\end{bmatrix}}_{W(\phi,\theta)}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\phi\\ \dot\theta\\ \dot\psi\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The attitude controller uses $W$ to turn Euler-rate commands into body-rate
commands ({class}`~drone_sim.control.pid.AttitudeController`). The plant needs
the inverse:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\phi\\ \dot\theta\\ \dot\psi\end{bmatrix} =
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
1 & \sin\phi\tan\theta & \cos\phi\tan\theta\\
0 & \cos\phi & -\sin\phi\\
0 & \sin\phi/\cos\theta & \cos\phi/\cos\theta
\end{bmatrix}}_{E(\phi,\theta) = W^{-1}}
\boldsymbol\omega .
$$

In code: {func}`~drone_sim.dynamics.plant.body_to_world_rotation` and
{func}`~drone_sim.dynamics.plant.euler_rate_matrix`.
::::

::::{dropdown} Proof 1: the kinematics blow up (det W = cos θ)
:icon: unfold

**Claim.** $W$ is singular exactly when $\cos\theta = 0$.

*Proof.* Expand along the first column:

$$
\det W = 1\cdot\big(\cos\phi\cdot\cos\phi\cos\theta + \sin\phi\cdot\sin\phi\cos\theta\big)
= \cos\theta\,(\cos^2\phi + \sin^2\phi) = \cos\theta. \qquad\square
$$

So $E = W^{-1}$ has $\det E = 1/\cos\theta$, and its entries contain
$\tan\theta$ and $1/\cos\theta$. As $\theta \to \pm 90^\circ$, a perfectly
ordinary, bounded body rate produces unbounded $\dot\phi$ and $\dot\psi$.
In the figure above, the constant body rate $\omega = [1, 0.2, 0.5]$ rad/s at
roll $10^\circ$ gives roll and yaw rates of about $\pm 17\,000$ °/s within
$0.1^\circ$ of vertical (the roll-rate curve is hidden under the yaw-rate
curve). Past $90^\circ$ the angle solver returns a different but equally valid
triple, $(\phi + 180^\circ, 180^\circ - \theta, \psi + 180^\circ)$.
::::

::::{dropdown} Proof 2: a degree of freedom disappears (only ψ − φ survives)
:icon: unfold

**Claim.** At $\theta = +90^\circ$, $R$ depends on $\phi$ and $\psi$ only
through $\psi - \phi$.

*Proof.* Conjugating a rotation moves its axis: for any rotation $A$,
$A\,R_{\mathbf n}(\alpha)\,A^\top = R_{A\mathbf n}(\alpha)$. Take
$A = R_y(90^\circ)$, which maps $\hat x$ to $-\hat z$:

$$
R_y(90^\circ)\,R_x(\phi)\,R_y(90^\circ)^\top = R_{-\hat z}(\phi) = R_z(-\phi).
$$

Hence $R_y(90^\circ)R_x(\phi) = R_z(-\phi)R_y(90^\circ)$, and

$$
R(\phi, 90^\circ, \psi) = R_z(\psi)\,R_z(-\phi)\,R_y(90^\circ) = R_z(\psi - \phi)\,R_y(90^\circ). \qquad\square
$$

(Similarly, at $\theta = -90^\circ$ only $\psi + \phi$ matters.) Roll and yaw
now rotate about the *same* axis: the "gimbals" have aligned. Converting a
matrix back to angles then has infinitely many answers. On a physical gimbal
(the Apollo 11 IMU famously guarded against it) the platform would actually
lock up; for numbers, the representation does.
::::

::::{dropdown} Why no choice of three angles can avoid this
:icon: unfold

Every other Euler sequence (XYZ, ZXZ, …) has the same problem at some angle.
This is a theorem: no three-parameter representation can cover all rotations
without a singularity (Stuelpnagel, *SIAM Review*, 1964). The space of
rotations, SO(3), is not topologically a piece of $\mathbb R^3$. The fix is
to use **four** parameters with one constraint, the unit quaternion, whose
kinematics $\dot q = \tfrac12 q \otimes [0, \boldsymbol\omega]$ are bounded
for every attitude.
::::

:::{admonition} Why it matters for the drone
:class: aerl-why
In the live demo, a person can point the board straight up. If the filters
used Euler angles, that pose would make the estimate spin wildly. Each part of
the project handles the problem differently:

| Component | Representation | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|
| Plant ({class}`~drone_sim.dynamics.plant.DronePlant`) | Euler angles | Flight stays far from vertical; it raises an error if it gets close |
| PID controller | Euler angles | Tilt commands are limited to $\pm 20^\circ$ |
| Madgwick, EKF | **Quaternions** | No singularity; handle any hand-held board pose |
| Live viewer heading | horizontal projection | {func}`~drone_sim.visualization.live_viewer.horizontal_heading` holds the last heading near vertical |
| Plots | Euler angles | Converted from quaternions for display only |
:::
